## 1) Consequential market, macroeconomic and business developments

### US rates cooled late in the week, but the bond-risk warning has not gone away

US stocks moved higher on 06 October, with S&P 500 and Nasdaq records helped by lower Treasury yields, lower oil and continuing AI optimism. That followed choppy trading earlier in the week when Reuters reported a global bond selloff had pushed US Treasury yields to multi-decade highs before yields reversed intraday. The implication for UK retail investors is two-sided: lower yields can support growth/technology valuations, but any renewed move higher in long yields would pressure equity multiples, bond funds and mortgage-sensitive sectors. ([uk.marketscreener.com](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/s-p-500-nasdaq-hit-record-highs-as-treasury-yields-stall-oil-slips-ce785dd9da8af226))

The Bank of England’s September Financial Policy Committee record, published within this briefing window, is directly relevant for UK portfolios: it said gilt and US Treasury yields had reached levels not seen since 2008, market adjustments had so far been mostly gradual, but hedge-fund leverage in the gilt market remained elevated and the risk of a sharp adjustment remained. That argues for caution on duration-heavy bond funds and leveraged/illiquid strategies, even if headline equity indices are rising. ([bankofengland.co.uk](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-policy-committee-record/2026/september-2026))

### US labour-market data lowered near-term Fed-hike pressure

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday 02 October, 13:30 BST, that September nonfarm payrolls rose **29,000**, unemployment was **4.2%**, average hourly earnings rose **0.1%** month on month and **3.0%** year on year, and July/August payrolls were revised down by **60,000** combined. That is a materially softer employment backdrop than the summer narrative and helps explain why markets became more comfortable with the Fed pausing in October. ([bls.gov](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm))

A key risk: softer jobs data is helpful for rate-sensitive equities only if it does not turn into an earnings recession. Watch banks, staffing, discretionary retail, travel and semiconductors for early signs of demand softness.

### US manufacturing still expanded, but input-price pressure worsened

ISM’s September manufacturing PMI, released Wednesday 01 October, 15:00 BST, was **54.5%**, its ninth consecutive month of expansion. New orders rose to **55.3%**, employment improved to **52.7%**, but the prices index jumped to **77.9%**, with ISM citing steel, aluminium, tariffs and petroleum-linked costs. ([ismworld.org](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/pmi/september/))

This is awkward for markets: activity is resilient, but cost pressure is not yet benign. Companies with pricing power and low leverage should be better placed than firms relying on cheap debt or margin recovery from falling input costs.

### UK Q2 GDP was revised higher; household income improved

ONS revised UK Q2 2026 real GDP growth to **0.5%**, up 0.1 percentage point from the first estimate, after **0.6%** growth in Q1. ONS also said real household disposable income per head rose **1.0%** in Q2 after a **0.8%** fall in Q1. ([ons.gov.uk](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproductgdp/bulletins/quarterlynationalaccounts/latest))

The balance-of-payments release showed the underlying current-account deficit, excluding precious metals, narrowed to **£11.3 billion**, or **1.4% of GDP**, in Q2. That is helpful for sterling-risk perceptions, but not enough on its own to remove UK fiscal and gilt-market concerns. ([ons.gov.uk](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/nationalaccounts/balanceofpayments/bulletins/balanceofpayments/apriltojune2026))

### Oil: OPEC+ held steady, emergency-stock headlines softened prices

OPEC said Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman agreed on 04 October to maintain September 2026 required production for November and to meet again on 01 November. ([opec.org](https://www.opec.org/pr-detail/1891616-4-october-2026.html))

Reuters reported on 05 October that oil prices slipped as Middle East crude exports increased and the G7 pledged additional supply releases, while conflict-linked disruption risk remained. For investors, lower oil helps airlines, logistics, consumer spending and inflation expectations, but energy-price volatility remains a live risk to central-bank policy and company margins. ([lse.co.uk](https://www.lse.co.uk/news/oil-steadies-as-middle-east-crude-exports-rise-and-g7-to-release-stocks-b90hbkd68c76ery.html))

### Tesla delivered a stronger operational print, but financial quality is still unknown

Tesla said on 02 October that Q3 2026 production was **464,391** vehicles, deliveries were **486,532**, and energy-storage deployments were **13.7 GWh**. Q3 financial results are due after the US close on Wednesday 21 October, with a Q&A webcast at 22:30 BST. ([ir.tesla.com](https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/tesla-third-quarter-2026-production-deliveries-and-deployments?trk=public_post_comment-text))

Deliveries exceeded production by **22,141** vehicles, suggesting inventory drawdown, but investors still need the financial release to judge average selling prices, automotive gross margin, regulatory credits, energy profitability and free cash flow.

## 2) Major earnings

### Micron Technology — fiscal Q4 2026, USD, GAAP and non-GAAP

Micron reported fiscal Q4 2026 results for the quarter ended 03 September 2026. Revenue was **$54.23bn**, up from **$41.46bn** in fiscal Q3 and **$11.32bn** a year earlier. GAAP net income was **$37.70bn**, or **$32.87 diluted EPS**; non-GAAP net income was **$38.40bn**, or **$33.42 diluted EPS**. GAAP gross margin was **86.8%** and operating cash flow was **$43.97bn**. Management guided fiscal Q1 2027 revenue to **$61.5bn ± $1.5bn**, with non-GAAP gross margin around **86.25%** and non-GAAP diluted EPS of **$38.15 ± $1.00**. ([investors.micron.com](https://investors.micron.com/news/press-release/2026/Micron-Technology-Inc--Reports-Record-Fiscal-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx))

**Implication:** Micron is the clearest earnings evidence this week that AI infrastructure demand is still feeding through to memory pricing and margins. **Risk:** memory is historically cyclical; these margins are exceptional, and any evidence of HBM/DRAM oversupply, hyperscaler capex discipline, or customer concentration would matter quickly.

### Nike — fiscal Q1 2027, USD, GAAP plus adjusted EPS guidance

Nike reported fiscal Q1 2027 results for the quarter ended 31 August 2026. Revenue was **$11.2bn**, down **4% reported** and **5% currency-neutral**. Gross margin rose **60 bps to 42.8%**; SG&A fell **3% to $3.9bn**; net income was **$0.7bn**, down **2%**; diluted EPS was **$0.48**. Nike said fiscal 2027 revenue is expected to decline **high single digits**, and adjusted diluted EPS is expected at **$1.15–$1.35**, excluding about **$0.15** of restructuring expense related to “Pace”. ([investors.nike.com](https://investors.nike.com/investors/news-events-and-reports/investor-news/investor-news-details/2026/NIKE-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2027-First-Quarter-Results/default.aspx))

**Implication:** the gross-margin improvement and cost control are positives, but declines in Greater China, EMEA, Nike Direct, Digital and Converse show the turnaround is not yet revenue-led. **Risk:** the market may focus on restructuring savings, but the investment case needs product momentum, channel health and China stabilisation, not just cost cuts.

### Lamb Weston — fiscal Q1 2027, USD, GAAP and adjusted/non-GAAP

Lamb Weston reported fiscal Q1 2027 results on 06 October. Net sales rose **1%** year on year to **$1.67bn**. Net income was **$29mn**, adjusted net income was **$103mn**, adjusted EBITDA was **$286mn**, reported diluted EPS was **$0.21** and adjusted diluted EPS was **$0.75**. Management raised fiscal 2027 targets: adjusted EBITDA to **$1.125bn–$1.215bn** and adjusted diluted EPS to **$3.05–$3.35**. ([investors.lambweston.com](https://investors.lambweston.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lamb-weston-announces-first-quarter-fiscal-2027-results-above))

**Implication:** the raise suggests execution is improving after a difficult period for frozen-potato volumes and costs. **Risk:** customer concentration, restaurant traffic, potato/input-cost inflation and food-service demand remain central.

### RPM International — fiscal Q1 2027, USD, GAAP and adjusted/non-GAAP

RPM reported record fiscal Q1 2027 sales of **$2.22bn**, up **4.8%** year on year. Net income was **$256.4mn**, diluted EPS was **$2.01**, adjusted diluted EPS was **$1.98**, and adjusted EBITDA was **$405.5mn**, up **4.5%**. For fiscal Q2, RPM projected sales and adjusted EBITDA to increase in the **low- to mid-single-digit** range; for the full year, it projected **mid-single-digit** sales and adjusted EBITDA growth. ([cmsrpminc.com](https://cmsrpminc.com/news/news-release/?workflowid=ff49c8f1-dd83-4cda-8ea9-2887328d8535))

**Implication:** coatings/building-products demand looks resilient rather than booming. **Risk:** higher rates can pressure repair/remodel and construction demand; raw-material and tariff costs remain watch items.
